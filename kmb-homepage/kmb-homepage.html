The condition

What is CCHS?

Congenital Central Hypoventilation Syndrome is an ultra-rare genetic condition, caused by a mutation in the PHOX2B gene, that disrupts the brain’s automatic control of breathing. When a child with CCHS falls asleep, their body simply forgets to breathe.

Every night of their lives, they depend on mechanical ventilation — through a tracheostomy or a mask — to keep them alive until morning.

CCHS for patients & parents CCHS for professionals
Watch: What is CCHS?
Also known as Ondine’s curse — a failure of the body’s drive to breathe, because it cannot sense oxygen and carbon dioxide levels.
Life support Lifelong ventilation every time they sleep — and for some children, when they are awake too.
Not just breathing CCHS is not just a respiratory disease. 50% of children with specific types of CCHS are born with or go on to develop tumours such as neuroblastoma, a type of cancer.
Treatment today None. There is no cure and no therapeutic option beyond artificial life support. That is what we are here to change.

Our mission

The research we’re funding

We are developing a treatment to change and to save the lives of CCHS patients — in a world where such a treatment does not exist. We will do whatever it takes. Our accelerated five-year plan is backed by over 130 doctors, scientists and medical professionals.

Treatment

The Breathing Pacemaker

A fully implantable biofeedback phrenic-nerve pacing system — the world’s first treatment designed specifically for CCHS. Like a cardiac pacemaker for breathing, it will sense what the body needs and adjust the rate and depth of every breath automatically — no wires, no external kit, no mechanical ventilator.

First patent filed

Monitoring

VENTO

The world’s smallest portable, non-invasive CO₂ wearable — built by Keep Me Breathing. VENTO reads the one signal that matters most to a child with CCHS — carbon dioxide — continuously, comfortably and without needles or hospital hardware.

Casper & Beau
