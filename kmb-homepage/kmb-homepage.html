The condition

What is CCHS?

Congenital Central Hypoventilation Syndrome is an ultra-rare genetic condition, caused by a mutation in the PHOX2B gene, that disrupts the brain’s automatic control of breathing. When a child with CCHS falls asleep, their body simply forgets to breathe.

Every night of their lives, they depend on mechanical ventilation — through a tracheostomy or a mask — to keep them alive until morning.

CCHS for patients & parents CCHS for professionals
Also known as Ondine’s curse — a failure of the body’s drive to breathe, because it cannot sense oxygen and carbon dioxide levels.
Life support Lifelong ventilation every time they sleep — and for some children, when they are awake too.
Treatment today None. There is no cure and no therapeutic option beyond artificial life support. That is what we are here to change.

Our mission

The research we’re funding

Keep Me Breathing is delivering an accelerated five-year plan — backed by over 130 doctors, scientists and medical professionals — to fund, develop and bring the world’s first CCHS-specific treatments to the children who need them.

Treatment

The Breathing Pacemaker

A fully implantable phrenic-nerve pacing system — the world’s first treatment designed specifically for CCHS. Like a cardiac pacemaker for breathing, it will sense what the body needs and adjust the rate and depth of every breath automatically, with no wires and no external kit.

Monitoring

VENTO

A pioneering wearable, non-invasive CO₂ monitor. VENTO is built to read the one signal that matters most to a child with CCHS — carbon dioxide — continuously, comfortably and without needles or hospital hardware.
